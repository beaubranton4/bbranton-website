# August 17, 2026

I haven't been super good about journaling, so I'd like to continue at least five minutes a day just talking, speaking, learning how to express my thoughts and get what's in my brain out onto a page — metaphorically speaking. The act of turning thoughts into something organized and speaking them audibly, I think, is a super valuable skill everybody needs to practice, and right now I don't get much social experience. I primarily speak at my job, which is remote and only requires one or two meetings a day, so I still barely talk to people. Luckily I have Abby to talk to, but sometimes we just speak in our own language of meeps and moops and sound effects, which isn't super ideal for learning how to be eloquent and well-spoken.

I need to expand my vocabulary and become a better public speaker. When I say public speaker, I think it's really just the skill of speaking, narrating, and enunciating — and there's a word that's at the tip of my tongue that I can't quite get, and those are the moments where I want to build the brain synapse connections so the words just fire away immediately, no searching for them. I'd like to speak with more tone, cadence, enthusiasm, and emotion — vary my speaking patterns, my volume, my pace, the emotion. The Ultra Speaking class I took led to really valuable experiences that improved my skills as a speaker, and I think I just need to keep practicing that.

Yesterday I went to Corte Madera Mall and lost my wallet. Did get my pants hemmed, though — have to pick them up Sunday, which is such a pain, going across the bridge three times in one week. That's $30 and three hours of my time, but that's what happens when you lose your wallet and mess up.

Had a good time at the Giants game this weekend and went back to Emporium — spent way too much money. These nights out are really hundred-dollar nights, so it's funny looking at a jacket's price tag and going "oh my gosh, it's $200" when I'll spend that on one or two nights out, often a whole weekend, sometimes more. I think I need to reframe my thinking: if I see a nice jacket I like and it's $100 more than any other jacket — just get the good one. Might as well love it. Not spending money for the sake of spending money, but there's no problem using your money to buy good quality. There's a reason it costs more, generally because it's better — you'll wear it more, like it more, get better use out of it, more durable.

That's one of the big things I'm working on: ditching the frugality habits ingrained in me by my parents. There's nothing wrong with being frugal — there's actually a lot of benefit to it — but there are limits, and there are instances where being frugal actually holds you back and hurts you. In life and in entrepreneurship, I think that's going to be one of the biggest things hurting me. I'll always be able to save, I'll make sure I have a profitable business, but I may not grow it as fast as I'd like because there's a third axis we're judged on. First axis is cost — how much we're spending. Second is how much we're making. But the third component, if you drew this in three-dimensional space, is time. If I can make $10,000 and only spend $2, that's a great gross margin — but if it took 300 years, nobody cares, that's not a business. If I can shrink that period of time, I'd much rather make $10 million in one year and spend $9.5 million doing it than the alternative.

This morning I logged into Claude and found out 100% of my usage limit had been reached, so I'm wondering if it's the automations I set up with the social media posting pipelines, or someone hacked my account, or a Claude bug — I really need to sort that out so I can get to work. Kind of funny, I'm completely dependent on Claude now, which I don't think is a good idea — something I should probably be less dependent on.
