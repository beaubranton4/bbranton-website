# July 24, 2026

I've come to realize that I really enjoy the process of research — analyzing, making a decision, and knowing full-heartedly that I've made the best possible decision I could make given the resources, the time, and the information I have. I really like comparison shopping. Yesterday I spent maybe two and a half, three hours just straight up looking at formal Indian wedding attire. I don't think many people have the focus and patience to browse dozens of sites, hundreds of listings, do the research. I think it's one of the things I actually enjoy and I'm actually pretty good at — something I'd never really pieced together, but it makes so much sense being a data analyst. I really do think patience is one of my superpowers, and it's what makes me good at this. It's also another reason I think I have the right skill set to build some sort of shopping or decision-making agent. Comparison shopping is really my bread and butter.

When I know I have an item to buy, that's really when I get into the weeds of things. I'm not just a shopper to shop — I know I need sunglasses, so I'm going to get the best possible pair, and I'll spend weeks and weeks researching, figuring out the best one, and pounce on the right deal. It's the same thing I did with the house here in San Francisco. It's the same thing I did with my office chair, my Steelcase Leap V2 — pounced on a $120 offer, drove all the way to Oakland to pick it up, after months and months of researching and waiting.

It's kind of funny, though — I'll admit and acknowledge that it wastes a lot of time. Sure, I probably saved $50, $100 doing all this research. I do get the best one, but oftentimes I already know what the best one is — I immediately know which one I want. It's just like trying to find that $20 savings, that white elephant, that white buffalo. It's the thrill of the chase. Something about it is exciting for me. I haven't been as productive as I'd like on Dugout Edge — that kind of took a backseat to the shopping experience.

I also figured out I could redeem my Amex points for way more cents on the dollar — almost two times the juice — if I redeemed them for gift cards instead of cash. Some of those gift cards are for Nordstrom, Lululemon, Banana Republic — that whole conglomerate: Old Navy, Banana Republic, Gap, and Athleta — which is great, because I have to shop for Cure de Mer's wedding. So I bought basically all of my outfits — a nice tan suit, some nice white linen pants, some dress shirts — pretty much all for free. I redeemed what probably would've been close to $180 in cash value and got the equivalent of $300 on the Banana Republic site. Super stoked about figuring that out, since I always just default to cash.

But that thing — I probably spent four hours figuring out what to do. Even valued in cash, I get those four hours back. I don't know, it's hard to say, but it feels good knowing it was a positive outcome, and that I found the most optimized outcome, financially speaking.

But that's really why I want to build Joe. I want to build something for people so they can get these outcomes — these financial outcomes — because there are so many ways to game the system with points and credit cards, but you have to take the time and the effort, and not many people are willing to do that. I think it's a process that can be 100% automated. It's just: figure out what you're already down to spend, what categories you're willing to spend in. Do I need to buy a plane ticket somewhere? Do I need to spend money at Lululemon? Was I going to do it anyway? That's when you get the real cost savings.
